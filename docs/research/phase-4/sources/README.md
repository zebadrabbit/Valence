# Sources of the aqueous reactions lab

Two manifests are in this folder.

`MANIFEST.json` lists every source of the lab's record (`../aqueous.json`) and of its primer (`src/chem-data/aqueous/primer.ts`): the address, the citation, and rows that say what the source prints and where. Each row names, in `fields`, the stored values it supports, by their place in the record with the item's id in brackets: `species[water].enthalpyOfFormation`, `solubility[silver-ion+chloride-ion]`, `stations[aq-02].notes[0]`. A row whose field is `primer` marks a source the primer rests on. Every source was fetched and read on 2026-10-06, but section 2.7 of OpenStax Chemistry 2e, which was fetched and read on 2026-10-07, when the names of the ions were added; its citation gives that date. Sections 2.6, 4.1, 4.2 and 18.5 of the book were fetched again that day, were byte for byte the files of the day before, and were read again for what was added.

`hazard-manifest.json` lists the safety data sheets behind the reagents' hazards (`../hazards.json`). It was written apart from the rest and keeps the form of the titration lab's manifest: one `field` to a row, beginning with the reagent's id. Three of its sheets are ones the titration lab cites too; the build lists each of those once.

## What is kept

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `pubchem-aqueous-reagents.json` | PubChem's molecular formula, molecular weight and title for the twelve compounds behind the fifteen reagents, from its PUG REST service |

It is its publisher's data, as downloaded and not edited by hand. It is not this project's work, and the repository's licence does not cover it. The build checks its sha256, each reagent's CID and atoms against it, and the molar mass of calcium carbonate and of magnesium.

Nothing else is kept. A value read from a page that is not kept rests on what the manifest says the page printed that day: the build cannot check it again, and a page that has changed since is not noticed. What the build does hold is that the record and the manifest give one account: every value that cites a source has a row of that source that names it, a value stored with the figure its source prints has a row that prints that figure, and no row names a value that does not cite its source.

The quotation fingerprint that `src/chem-data/aqueous/quotation.test.ts` compares the lab's words against is not kept either, for the reason `../../phase-3/sources/README.md` gives. `scripts/fingerprint-sources.mjs` writes it to this folder, where `.gitignore` lists it, from pages saved outside the repository:

    node scripts/fingerprint-sources.mjs <folder of saved pages> <YYYY-MM-DD> docs/research/phase-4/sources

## How each kind of value was read

- **Solubility of each pair of ions, and how equations are written.** OpenStax Chemistry 2e, located by section and heading and never quoted: Table 4.1 of section 4.2 for the solubility of each pair, section 4.1 for the three forms of an equation. The record stores one row for each pair the ten stations can bring together; the manifest has one row for each line of the table, naming the pairs read from it. Spec 5.2 names a handbook for these values; the table of a general chemistry text was used instead, as the phase's instructions asked.
- **Activity series.** One entry, magnesium above hydrogen, from the table of standard reduction potentials in section 17.3 of the same book. As the text of the page was taken out of it for reading, that table's row gives the ion as Mg2(aq), without the sign of its charge; the two electrons of the half-reaction give the charge.
- **Enthalpies of formation.** The NBS tables (Wagman and others, 1982), as spec 5.2 names them, read as the scanned PDF that NIST serves. The file has no text layer: pages 2-38 (Table 2, hydrogen) and 2-260 (Table 93, magnesium) were turned into images and read by eye. Six values are stored, of which four are zero.
- **Heat capacity and density of the liquid.** Water's, from the worked example of section 5.2 of the book in which hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide solution are mixed. The section prints 4.184 J/(g °C); the record stores the 4.18 of spec 5.2.
- **Colours, textures and odours.** One source to a row, or two where the colour is read in one place and the texture in another. The sources are a set of pages on the characteristic reactions of metal ions, sections 18.5 and 19.1 of the book, one page of Chemguide, a university's instruction sheet for preparing basic copper carbonate, and the appearance lines of four Wikipedia infoboxes. The Wikipedia pages are secondary sources, cited by revision.
- **Molar masses and the identity of each reagent.** PubChem's kept table.
- **Names of the ions, and of water and the three gases.** OpenStax Chemistry 2e: the table of polyatomic ions and the text of section 2.6, the naming rules of section 2.7, the worked example of section 4.2 and section 18.5. A name is stored for each ion that a bottle gives or an equation of a station holds, as it is said before the word "ion", and for water, carbon dioxide, ammonia and hydrogen. The compounds and the solids are not named here: each reagent has its name from PubChem's table, and the name of a solid that forms is the learner's to work out.
- **Hazards.** `hazard-manifest.json`.

## What could not be sourced, or only in part

- **The hex value of each ion's colour** is this project's choice for drawing the colour its source names. No source gives one.
- **The colour of the mixed liquid at station 5**, stored as orange, is this project's name for the blend of a pink solution and a yellow-orange one. No source for the mixture was found. The engine reports the two coloured ions; the name of the blend is curated.
- **Blue-green at station 2 and pale green at station 3** are the colours of the basic carbonates that really form, read from a description of the dried solid in each case, and stored under the simple carbonates the stations' equations write. No source describing the fresh precipitate in the tube was read. The two sources read on copper disagree about whether carbon dioxide is given off as the solid forms (the manifest's row for `chemguide-carbonates` says how), and the station's note says nothing of the gas. The source read for basic nickel carbonate makes it from nickel sulfate; the station uses the chloride.
- **Rust-brown** is the word of spec 8.2 for a solid one source calls red-brown and another, read and not cited, orange-brown. **Yellow-orange** is the spec's word for solutions a source calls yellow or amber.
- **Nickel(II) and cobalt(II)** are not printed in the section their names cite. Each is formed by that section's rule, a Roman numeral for the charge of a metal that forms ions of more than one charge, from the charge the record stores. **Silver** is stored without a numeral, as the name of its compound has it; the book writes silver(I) once, in section 4.1.
- **The primer's card of worked steps.** Its three equations, the two counts of its fifth step and the formula KCl are worked out on the card by the methods of the sections it cites, and are printed in none of them; tests make the same counts. The formula of calcium phosphate and the name of K3PO4 are printed (sections 2.6 and 2.7), and the solubility of its two new pairs is read from Table 4.1. Three things on the card rest on no passage and are marked as the lab's own in its support lines: that the positive ion is written first in a formula, which both sections do and neither states; that a group of atoms that is the same on both sides may be counted as one thing; and that steps five to eight are the same where a gas or water forms in place of a solid. In the first card, that a solution of a base is called basic has no source.
- **No texture** is stored for silver chloride or for the two carbonates: none of the sources read gives one for the precipitate.
- **Ammonium and hydroxide.** Table 4.1 read line by line gives two answers for this pair: soluble by its row for ammonium, insoluble by its row for hydroxide, which does not list ammonium among its exceptions. The pair is stored as soluble. In the lab the two ions react by another rule.
- **The temperature classes.** Warm from 5 °C and hot from 25 °C are model parameters, each with its rationale in the record. Nothing was tuned to the temperature rises of spec 8.4: 40.07 °C at station 8 and 11.03 °C at station 10 are what the stored enthalpies, heat capacity, density and amounts give.
- **Heat at the other eight stations** is not worked out. Spec 8.1 gives heat to two rules, neutralisation and a metal with an acid, and the model follows it; the enthalpies of the precipitations were not read.
- **The laboratory manual cited for how to waft** has no author on the page that was read. It is cited by its title and by where it was read.

## The hazard notes since 2026-10-18

The fifteen notes under the reagents' statements (`../hazards.json`) were rewritten on 2026-10-18 into the form the later labs' notes have (spec 5.2): three short paragraphs of 160 words at most. Five of them are the records of other labs' bottles too (the 1 M and 6 M hydrochloric acid, the 1 M and 6 M sodium hydroxide and the iron(III) chloride, which the calorimetry, Group I cations, Le Chatelier's principle, iodination of acetone and types of reactions labs carry unchanged), so each note says only what holds on every bench that shows it.

- **The first paragraph** gives the bench and what the supplier's sheet for that strength, or the nearest sheet where none covers it, gives.
- **The second** says what the lab goes by. It is headed "Stricter, and what this lab goes by" where that is a stricter document: another of the record's own sheets (the sheet for the next strength up or down, or the sheet for the solid where the solution's sheet does not reach the bench's strength or asks less) or, since the second pass below, a stricter document that a later lab read for the same substance. It is headed "What this lab goes by" where the first paragraph's sheet is itself what the lab goes by: since the second pass, only the 1 M sodium hydroxide and the calcium carbonate.
- **The third** says that disposal is decided by the laboratory's own rules and local regulations and to ask the laboratory's supervisor, and gives no method. Each note's first sheet has a row for its section on disposal, which names one of the supplier's numbered methods; no note gives it.

No signal word, statement or heading changed. In the first pass every fact of a note was from a sheet the record already cited, read again from the copy saved on the day it was read (each checked against the hash in the manifest) and not fetched again. The notes no longer give a strength by mass: the rows that printed a percentage say what the bench's strength is by mass and that the note gives none.

**Read and not carried** (rows whose field ends in `notCarried`): that calcium carbonate gives off carbon dioxide in acid, and the sheet's line keeping it from acids, since that is what station 7 shows; that concentrated acid gives off heat when diluted, since station 8 asks whether its tube warms; what the sheets for the solids of ammonium nitrate, copper(II) sulfate and silver nitrate keep them from, beyond what the notes give, since none of it meets them on this bench; and the dry ammonium nitrate's explosion on heating or grinding, since the bench holds a dilute solution. A test holds every note to naming no gas, no heat, no solid that forms and no colour that a station shows.

### The stricter documents of later labs, carried (the second pass of 2026-10-18)

The first pass took its facts only from the documents this record cited. Later labs had read stricter documents about seven of the same substances, and by the rule of 2026-10-08 the stricter of the documents read is what a lab shows; so, later the same day, the notes of those seven took them, each as the lab that read it applies it, where the document is about the same substance in a form or strength it can speak for. Thirteen documents were added to `hazard-manifest.json`, each with the address and citation the other lab gives it, read again from the copy that lab saved (checked against the recorded hash) and not fetched again, with rows of their own for this lab's bottles and rows of what was read and not carried.

- **Silver nitrate, 0.1 M** (from the Group I cations lab, whose bottle is the same strength): a second supplier's sheet for a 0.1 M solution adds H400, very toxic to water life, so the solution is kept out of drains and waterways; eyes flushed for 15 minutes or more and a doctor at once; if any is swallowed, a doctor or a poison centre at once. A third supplier's sheet for the solid adds harm to an unborn child.
- **Barium chloride, 0.5 M** (from the flame test lab, whose bottle is 0.1 M): the second supplier's sheet for 0.1 M asks no breathing of the mist, 15 minutes at the eyewash with a doctor at once and a call at once if any is swallowed; a sheet for a weaker solution is taken to hold at least as much for this one, as the types of reactions lab takes one for calcium chloride. A third's for the solid adds serious irritation of the eyes. NOAA's record has barium compounds on the EPA's list, and the solution is kept out of drains and waterways, as the flame test lab has it. That record's standing first aid of 20 to 30 minutes is not carried, as the flame test lab does not carry it.
- **Copper(II) sulfate, 0.5 M** (from the types of reactions lab, whose bottle is 1 M): NOAA's record of the solid has the eyes flushed for 20 to 30 minutes with a hospital or a poison centre called meanwhile, and the skin flooded with water and washed with soap.
- **Ammonium nitrate, 0.5 M** (from the calorimetry lab, whose bottle is the solid): NOAA's line that clothing it gets on can be a fire risk once dry, and the second supplier's sheet's lines on taking such clothing off and washing it and on 15 minutes at the eyewash with a doctor at once. NOAA's mark of the chemical as explosive is not carried: it is of the dry solid, and this bench holds a solution of about 4%.
- **Cobalt(II) chloride, 0.5 M** (from the Le Chatelier's principle lab, whose bottle is the solid): the solution is handled by the sheets for the solid, as it was, so the two stricter sheets for the solid speak for it too: the second supplier's classes the solid as corrosive (H314) and very toxic to water life (H410), so the solution is kept out of drains and waterways; the third's adds harm if breathed in and a fume hood only; both ask 15 minutes for the eyes and the skin with a doctor at once. The corrosive class is the question `../../lechatelier/README.md` sets out for the owner, and goes with whatever is decided there. The note no longer says that no sheet gives first aid for the eyes.
- **Magnesium ribbon** (from the types of reactions lab): NOAA's record has a magnesium fire never fought with water, foam or a carbon dioxide extinguisher. Its line that the metal gives off hydrogen with dilute acid is not carried, since that is what station 10 shows; nor its line on the flame, since nothing is burned on this bench.
- **Hydrochloric acid, 1 M, 3 M and 6 M** (from the Le Chatelier's principle lab): the second supplier's sheet for 0.5 M acid, weaker than any of the three, asks 15 minutes at the eyewash and a doctor at once, taken to hold at least as much here; NOAA's record of the acid adds 15 minutes more where no doctor has come. Its lines that the acid gives off hydrogen with metals and reacts violently with sodium hydroxide are not carried, since those are what stations 10 and 8 show; nor the sheet's milder class, since the first supplier's sheets are the stricter on the hazard. The 1 M and the 6 M acid are the records of five other labs, which take the change with them.
- **No other document** was read by any lab for the iron(III) chloride, the sodium hydroxide, the sodium carbonate, the nickel chloride or the calcium carbonate; their notes are as the first pass left them.

The notes keep to 160 words and three paragraphs, and the test that no note says what a station shows still holds them. The primer's card on a splash now says that the supplier's sheets give several minutes and that these documents ask 15 minutes or more with a doctor (20 to 30 for copper(II) sulfate), and names each. The no-quotation fingerprint of this folder now holds those thirteen pages too, from the copies the other labs saved:

    node scripts/fingerprint-sources.mjs <folder of the 57 pages and the 13 copies> 2026-10-07 docs/research/phase-4/sources

One question from before is moot: the supplier's sheet for a 2.5% cobalt chloride solution carries the usual words for rinsing the eyes, and the note now gives the stricter first aid of the sheets for the solid.
