# The deep review at the close of phase 3

2026-10-05. The one review of the whole branch that `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-04-phase-3-titration-lab.md` puts at the end of the phase ("How the phase was run from Task 14", and Task 23), made on the branch at `527d17b`, when the screens and the documents were done. This file records what it found, what was done about each finding, and what nobody checked. It is kept short, in the way `gate-a.md` records Gate A.

**Where it stands.** Fourteen findings, one must-fix and thirteen should-fix, and sixteen notes. All fourteen are fixed and tested. One finding has a second half that is the owner's to decide and is not built (DR2). The notes are recorded with what was done about each, which for most is nothing. **The fixes have not been checked by anyone who did not make them**, and no person has looked at the page since.

## How the review was done, as far as this record knows

The findings came to the fix wave as a list, each with where it is, what is wrong, the evidence and a proposed fix. From that evidence: the reviewers drove the production build under `vite preview` in headless Chromium, by keyboard, at widths of 700, 1366, 1500, 1920 and 2200 pixels; watched every `role="status"` and `role="alert"` node for changes after each key press; ran the whole test suite three times, once with the quotation fingerprint moved aside as CI has it; and read the code of the screens. They were told not to judge how anything looks, and not to read the primer, the hazard notes and the wording files sentence by sentence again. Their scripts and outputs are not in the repository.

## How the fixes were made

Each finding was reproduced before it was touched. A throwaway script drove a build of `527d17b` in Chromium, on stored records made with the engine's test kit, and showed the defect for every finding that shows in a browser. Each fix that is logic or markup has a test that was seen failing first. One commit per finding. The same script was then run on the build with all the fixes, and showed none of the defects; and the scripts that had driven a whole Guided lab and the checks of the page when the screens were built were run again on that build.

### The check of the tabs, and what was done

The same day the redesign was checked by a reviewer who had not built it, in headless Chromium on a production build at 1024×768, at 820×1180 by touch and at 1366×768. Three findings were to be fixed and were, each reproduced first in the browser; six notes are for the record. The fixes were checked by a script that is not kept in the repository: 123 checks, all passing on a production build and on the development server, of which 62 failed on the page as it was.

**Fixed.**

1. **Start, pressed from the Primer tab, left the page scrolled deep into the Lab panel or at its very end.** At 1024×768 the checklist's line was 884 px above the window and the whole shelf was out of view; at 820×1180 by touch the line was 606 px above it. The builder's report, that the panel came to rest just under the bar, was true only of the second Start button, the one on the Lab tab. The cause: Base UI keeps the panel that is being left laid out for one more frame, inert, with the next panel under it, for a transition out. Start moved focus to the Lab panel in that frame, the browser scrolled to it where it then lay, below the whole primer card, and a frame later the card was hidden and the page shrank. How far down it landed followed the height of the topic that was showing. Now: the panel being left is out of the layout at once, since the page has no transition out; the focus is moved without scrolling; and the window is put with the tab bar at its top. Start from above the tabs and from the Lab tab, by keyboard, pointer and touch, with topic 1 and with topic 7 showing, at the three sizes: the bar at the top, the checklist's line at 65 px, the top of the shelf at 140 px, focus on the panel.
2. **Coming back to the Lab tab always showed it from its top.** The stream did stop on leaving and the sentence was there on return, but at 1024×768 the stopcock's button was at 994 px in a 768 px window and the sentence under it, and every look at the primer in the middle of a titration lost the place on the bench. Now each tab is shown again where it was left: when a tab is left with its panel scrolled under the bar, how far is kept while the page is open, and the tab comes back to that place. A tab not yet seen, or left while the bar was not stuck, is shown from the top of its panel where the bar is stuck and is not moved where it is not, which is what every tab did before. Restart forgets the Lab tab's place. On return the stopcock's button was within a pixel of where it was left and the message line was in the window at all three sizes; the Primer came back to its own place likewise; the place was kept by keyboard, with focus still on the tab.
3. **"This lab measures agreement by the relative deviation." was set off as a rule of the simulation alone.** Relative deviation in parts per thousand is a measure of agreement that a real bench uses too, so the sentence states the lab's choice and no departure from a bench, and set off with the others it told a reader skimming for what holds at a bench the opposite. It is plain now; the term is still bold. Eleven sentences are set off, in eight paragraphs and nine blocks. That the measure is an ordinary one at a bench is the reviewer's and the fixer's judgement: the primer's own support line for that paragraph says no source was read for it, and none was read here.

One rule, `tabShift` in `LabShell.tsx`, says how far the window moves when a tab is shown, and `shell.test.ts` holds it, with the class that takes a panel out of the layout as it is left. 2235 tests in 72 files.
