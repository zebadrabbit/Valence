# Valence

An independent, browser-based molecule explorer and virtual general-chemistry lab for self-directed learners.

Valence recreates common general chemistry bench labs so anyone can perform them, understand the chemistry, and check their own results. It is a personal learning project with no connection to any school.

## Status

All five phases of v1 are built. Phase 1 is the foundation: the app shell, themes, guest progress storage, and the chemistry-data validator. Phase 2 is the molecule explorer: molecules in 3D and as Lewis structures, with atom, bond and measurement cards, three valence display levels, and a Ctrl+K search. Its library was 31 molecules and is 101 since 2026-10-09: small molecules and ions, a gallery of VSEPR shapes, organic functional groups, biomolecules, and everyday molecules such as caffeine and vanillin, with a field on the library page that filters them by name or formula. The explorer also has a periodic table of all 118 elements, added after phase 2: coloured by chemical family or by a property, with a card of sourced values for each element and links to and from the molecules. Phase 3 is the titration lab: a sodium hydroxide solution is standardized against hydrochloric acid on a drawn bench, in a Guided or a Challenge mode, each trial is checked against a seeded model, and the run is still there after a reload. Phase 4 is the aqueous reactions lab: ten fixed stations, each a test tube in which two reagents are mixed, observed by eye and with tools, and written up as a molecular, a complete ionic and a net ionic equation built from a palette of formulas, in a Guided or a Challenge mode, with a solubility table, a table of the bench's ions and a calculator that float over the page. Each lab needs a window at least 768 pixels wide. Phase 5 is the mechanism timeline: hydrochloric acid and sodium hydrogen carbonate followed atom by atom through seven chapters, in a 3D scene that is played, stepped through or scrubbed, with a panel that draws free energy and enthalpy along the way and says which of its values are cited, which derived and which only drawn, and a Challenge mode that asks what happens next before each step.

One lab has been added since v1, the flame test lab: six stations, one for each of six metal chlorides in solution. At a station a wire loop is cleaned, dipped and held in a drawn burner's flame; the flame is looked at, then its light through a hand spectroscope, a strip with a scale from 400 to 780 nm on which sharp lines and broad bands show; and the colour of the flame, where the brightest thing is and whether it is a line or a band are recorded from fixed choices, in a Guided or a Challenge mode. An observation made with a loop that was not clean does not count. A closing question asks what gives each flame its colour. Its screens were built on 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09 and have been checked by script by the AI coding assistant that built them; the review by one that did not build them was held on 2026-10-09, by script in the same way, and what it changed is in the record of the build. No person has run the lab, and no chemist has read it.

A second lab was added on 2026-10-09, the calorimetry lab: a foam-cup calorimeter with a lid, a stirrer and a temperature probe whose logger draws temperature against time. It has three parts: the heat given out when hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide solution are mixed, the heat taken in when ammonium nitrate dissolves, and the specific heat of an unknown metal, which is one of four. In each the learner sets up a drawn cup, starts the change, lets time pass, looks at the readings on the graph, and fills in a worksheet in which every entry must follow from their own earlier entries, in a Guided or a Challenge mode. A run counts only if the lid was on and the cup stirred from the start of the change and its result is within 5% of the figure the lab holds; a part can be run again, with new numbers. Its screens were built on 2026-10-09 and were checked by script by the AI coding assistant that built them. The review of the whole lab was held the same day by an AI coding assistant that had not built it, working by script in headless Chromium: it reworded what each part says it finds, so that Challenge can be shown it, put the bench's figures at the head of the notebook, and put right what it found, which the research record lists. No person has run the lab, no screen reader was used, one browser was tried and no real device, and no chemist or chemistry instructor has read it.

A third lab was added on 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10, the Group I cations lab: qualitative analysis of a solution for silver, lead and mercury(I), the three ions whose chlorides do not dissolve in cold dilute acid. It has two parts, a known that holds all three and an unknown that holds one, two or all three, each worked through five steps on three drawn test tubes: hydrochloric acid, hot water, chromate, ammonia, and nitric acid with litmus paper. At each step the learner adds the reagent, heats or centrifuges, looks at the liquid and at the bottom of the tube to find what is there, and records it from fixed choices; in part 1 they also fill a flow chart of the scheme from tiles, and in part 2 they name the unknown's ions at the end, in a Guided or a Challenge mode. A run does not count if the liquid was poured off before the solid was centrifuged, if the water of step 2 was poured off cold, or if step 5 was recorded before litmus showed the liquid acid. Its screens were built on 2026-10-10 and were checked by script, in headless Chromium, by the AI coding assistant that built them. The one deep review was held the same day, by an AI coding assistant that had built none of the lab, working by script in headless Chromium and by reading: it made the engine refuse a naming of none of the three ions, gave room to three disclosures whose hit areas lay over one another, put the centrifuge before the record in three steps' tasks, and left the drawing as it is, with what it measured of it. What it found, changed and left is the last section of the lab's research record. No person has run the lab, no screen reader was used, one browser was tried and no real device, and no chemist or chemistry instructor has read it.

A fourth lab was added on 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11, the Le Chatelier's principle lab: four equilibria, each in a drawn test tube, disturbed by adding something, taking something away, heating or cooling. They are lead chloride with its ions, the two forms of methyl violet indicator, the pink and the blue ion of cobalt, and the hydroxides of zinc and of magnesium, seventeen disturbances between them. At each the learner first says which way the equilibrium will shift, which is kept and never marked; then makes the change, a portion at each press; looks at the liquid and at the bottom of the tube to find what is there; records it from fixed choices; and says which way it went, or at four tubes what took the metal ion away. The disturbance's notes then say what happened and why, with the learner's prediction and answer over them, in a Guided or a Challenge mode. A system counts when each observation is what its tube showed and each answer is right, and a system that does not is worked again. Nothing in it is drawn by chance. Its screens were built on 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11 and were checked by script, in headless Chromium, by the AI coding assistant that built them; the one review, by an assistant that had built none of the lab, was held on 2026-10-11 in the same way, and what it found, changed and left is the last section of the lab's research record. No person has run the lab, no screen reader was used, one browser was tried and no real device, and no chemist or chemistry instructor has read it.

A fifth lab was added on 2026-10-10 and 2026-10-11, the clock reaction lab: bromate, iodide and acid, with a little thiosulfate and starch, in a drawn flask that stays clear and colourless and then turns dark blue all at once. The learner makes the two flasks of a mixture ready, pours one into the other, which starts a watch, and stops the watch at the change; the page plays the time faster than a clock, the five mixtures at one speed so that a shorter time is a shorter wait, and the time recorded is the model's with the learner's own delay added, or the watch is stopped at the change for a learner who asks for that. Five mixtures are timed at room temperature and the first again in ice water, cool water and warm water. From the times a notebook of typed entries, checked as the calorimetry lab's are, finds the order in each of the three reactants and the rate constant, and from a graph that is explored the activation energy, in a Guided or a Challenge mode; the learner works one row by hand and the notebook works the rest and says so. The orders and the rate constant are cited; the activation energy the model runs on, 50 kJ/mol, is the lab's own figure and not a measured one, and the lab says so. It runs on a kinetics engine that takes a reaction's record, which a second lab is to share. Its screens were built on 2026-10-11 and were checked by script, in headless Chromium, by the AI coding assistant that built them; the one review, by an assistant that had built none of the lab, was held on 2026-10-11 in the same way, and what it changed, found and left is the last section of the lab's research record. No person has run the lab, no screen reader was used, one browser was tried and no real device, and no chemist or chemistry instructor has read it.

## Development

Requires Node 22.22 or a later 22 release, Node 24, or Node 26 or newer. Node 23 is not supported by the linter or the test runner, and Node 25 is not supported by the test runner.

    npm install
    npm run dev        # start the app locally
    npm test           # run the tests, including chem-data validation
    npm run lint       # architecture rules: chemistry code stays pure and deterministic
    npm run build      # lint, test, type-check, and build to dist/; a failure at any step stops the build
    npm run build:data # regenerate src/chem-data/ JSON from docs/research/phase-2/, docs/research/periodic-table/, docs/research/phase-3/, docs/research/phase-4/, docs/research/emission-spectra/, docs/research/flame/, docs/research/calorimetry/, docs/research/cations/ and docs/research/lechatelier/, and the mechanism's record in src/mechanisms/ from docs/research/phase-5/
    npm run build:expected # regenerate the expected-values test fixture from docs/research/phase-2/expected.json
    npm run cross-check # rewrite docs/research/periodic-table/cross-check.md from the saved sources

To have the validator reject specific institution names, create `src/chem-data/denylist.local.json` containing a JSON array of names. The file is ignored by git.

## v1 scope

- A molecule explorer with atom and bond inspection over a curated library of about 30 molecules (grown to 101 after v1's phases were built), and a periodic table (added to the scope after the explorer was built).
- Two labs: standardization of a base by titration, and ten reactions in aqueous solution. (A third, flame tests, a fourth, calorimetry, a fifth, the Group I cations, a sixth, Le Chatelier's principle, and a seventh, the clock reaction, were added after v1.)
- One mechanism timeline: HCl + NaHCO₃.
- Guest-only; progress is saved in the browser.

Every chemistry value in the app has a cited source. The explorer's values are hand-curated and covered by tests. The periodic table's values are built by script from saved copies of PubChem, CIAAW and NIST data, cross-checked, with a note on the card wherever a value needs one. The titration lab's constants come from IAPWS, NIST, the NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, PubChem, a paper in Analytical Sciences, a supplier's catalogue page and two Wikipedia pages; the hazard statements on its bottles are those of Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, with a note from a safety data sheet for the solution at the strength used (the indicator's from Ricca Chemical Company); and its primer rests on OpenStax Chemistry 2e, David Harvey's Analytical Chemistry 2.1 and pages on laboratory technique, quoting none of them. The safety data sheets and most of the other pages were read and are not kept, so the build cannot check the lab's record against them again. The aqueous reactions lab's values come from OpenStax Chemistry 2e (the solubility of each pair of ions and the forms of an equation), the NBS tables of 1982 (enthalpies of formation), PubChem, pages on the reactions of metal ions, Chemguide and Wikipedia (colours and odours), and Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets for its fifteen reagents, read and not kept. The mechanism timeline's energies are worked out by the build from the NBS tables of 1982 and from the acid constant of carbonic acid in a paper of 2016, read and not kept; where its atoms are between the species' library shapes is Valence's own choreography. The flame test lab's atomic lines are the NIST Atomic Spectra Database's, from saved tables; its bands are from a paper of 1975, the English abstracts of two papers of 1979 and a doctoral dissertation; its flame colours from Chemguide and an open textbook; and the hazards of its six bottles from Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, with what a second or a third supplier's sheet or a NOAA record says more strictly of the substance, all read and not kept. Which of a flame's lines and bands the lab calls the brightest follows what those sources' instruments recorded, which is not always what an eye would find. The calorimetry lab's two enthalpies are worked out from the enthalpies of formation of the NBS tables of 1982, in the rows for solutions of about the strength on the bench, and its four metals' specific heats from the same tables' heat capacities and the CIAAW's standard atomic weights; its specific heat and density of water are the NIST Chemistry WebBook's, given to every solution too, as an idealisation; the hazards of its ammonium nitrate and of its four metals are from Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, with what another supplier's sheet or a NOAA record says more strictly of the substance, all read and not kept; and how fast its cup trades heat with the room is the lab's own model, not a measurement. The Group I cations lab works out the solubility products of its three chlorides and the formation constant of the silver-ammonia ion from the Gibbs energies of the NBS tables of 1982; the solubility of lead chloride cold and hot is from a page on the reactions of the lead ion, and the solubility product of lead chromate from a university's handout that gives no source for it; its scheme, its rules and what each solid and liquid looks like are from an open textbook of qualitative analysis, pages on the reactions of metal ions, a college's experiment, a university's handout and a college's pre-lab notes; and the hazards of its eight bottles are from Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, and for mercury(I) nitrate from Fisher Scientific's, with what another supplier's sheet or a NOAA record says more strictly of the substance, all read and not kept. How much of anything is in a tube is the lab's own model, with amounts that are fixed. The Le Chatelier's principle lab works out six constants (the solubility products of zinc hydroxide and of magnesium hydroxide, the formation constants of the two complex ions of zinc, the constant of ammonia as a base and the ion product of water) from the Gibbs energies of the NBS tables of 1982, and the heat that dissolving lead chloride takes in from their enthalpies; the solubility of lead chloride cold and hot is from a page on the reactions of the lead ion, and methyl violet's range from a reference table of indicators; its procedure is a college's handout's; the colours of the two cobalt ions, hot and cold, are from a demonstration of the Nuffield Foundation and the Royal Society of Chemistry and a page of Chemguide, and its other rules from pages on the reactions of metal ions and an open textbook of qualitative analysis; and the hazards of eight of its ten bottles are from Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, with what another supplier's sheet or a NOAA record says more strictly of the substance, all read and not kept, the other two bottles carrying the aqueous reactions lab's records. Where its numbers can decide what a tube shows, the build checks them at the lab's amounts; the cobalt tubes are stored observations; the colours on the screen are the drawing's own, and the middle of methyl violet's range, which no source read names, is drawn between its blue and its yellow. The clock reaction lab's orders are from the summary of a kinetic study of the reaction of bromate with iodide and from the summary of a thesis, which also gives its rate constant; its mixtures and its procedure are from four schools' and colleges' handouts for this experiment; its two constants are NIST's; its primer rests on those and on pages of Chemguide; and the hazards of its five bottles are from Flinn Scientific's safety data sheets, with what another supplier's sheet, a state's fact sheet or a NOAA record says more strictly of the substance, all read and not kept. Its activation energy is cited to nothing: it is the lab's own figure, and the times of its runs are its model's. None of these publishers is connected with Valence or has reviewed it. The About page lists every source.
